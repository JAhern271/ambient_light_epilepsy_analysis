# Methodological Standard for a Cross-Sectional NHANES 2011–2014 Analysis of Ambient Light, Rest–Activity Rhythms and Sleep in People With Epilepsy

## TL;DR
- The single direct precedent is Tang, Zhou & Zhai (2024, *Front Neurol* 15:1496507): it used NHANES 2013–2014, defined epilepsy from the prescription file (ICD-10 G40 + manual antiseizure-medication screen), found only 53 cases among 7,410, and derived IS/IV/RA/M10/L5 with nparACT — but it never analysed PAXLUX light, never modelled sleep, and did not report using SDMVSTRA/SDMVPSU or the correct MEC accelerometry weight. **No published study has analysed the NHANES PAXLUX ambient-light channel as a circadian exposure in adults**, so the light arm of your paper is genuinely novel and must borrow its entire metric/reporting framework from UK Biobank and the Spitschan/CIE light-metrology literature.
- The biggest defensibility threats are structural, not fixable by better modelling: (a) NHANES 2011–2014 collected no bedtime/waketime and no sleep diary, so sleep timing must be inferred from the same wrist accelerometer that produces both the activity and light metrics; (b) this creates a three-way shared-device tautology (a wrist under bedding reads near-zero movement AND near-zero lux by construction); and (c) cross-sectional mediation cannot identify the light→circadian→sleep ordering. Each must be pre-empted with sensitivity analyses and honest "attenuation" (not "mediation") language.
- Recommended: run a conservative survey-weighted primary analysis (light and rest–activity rhythm as co-primary outcome families, epilepsy defined by ASM+G40, WTMEC2YR with Taylor linearisation, E-values) plus a matched-cohort sensitivity analysis. Treat sleep as a secondary/exploratory domain, not a co-equal third outcome family — adding it imports a fresh circularity and a correlated outcome family that will likely dilute rather than strengthen the paper.

## Key Findings

1. **PAXLUX has essentially no adult analytic precedent.** The only published NHANES light-sensor analyses use the 2012 NNYFS child sample to validate lux as an indoor/outdoor discriminator (Flynn et al.; the NNYFS preschool paper, PMC9040315). No adult NHANES paper has used PAXLUX as a circadian light-exposure exposure, and the sensor has never been validated against reference photometry. CDC's own NHANES 2011–2014 PAXLUX documentation states the lux estimates "are only estimates and are not meant for exact interpretation of the light detected by the device… only intended as a general guideline for average light intensities."

2. **The direct precedent (Tang 2024) is a partial template only.** It establishes that (i) epilepsy is ascertainable in NHANES only via prescription data, (ii) nparACT-derived nonparametric indices are publishable, and (iii) the case count is very small (n=53). It does not address light, sleep, the complex-survey design fully, or the confounding structure your brief demands.

3. **NHANES 2011–2014 sleep self-report is minimal and lacks timing.** SLQ_G and SLQ_H contain only three items — SLD010H (usual weekday/workday sleep hours), SLQ050 (ever told doctor had trouble sleeping) and SLQ060 (ever told had a sleep disorder). Bedtime/waketime (SLQ300/SLQ310) and weekend items were only added in 2015–2016 (adapted from the Munich ChronoType Questionnaire). There was no sleep diary or log during the PAM protocol. This forces diary-free sleep estimation and rules out social-jetlag/chronotype metrics from self-report.

4. **Defensible, validated NHANES-specific sleep pipelines already exist** — Thapa-Chhetry et al. (2022, *MSSE*) validated a wake-wear/sleep-wear/nonwear classifier against PSG on the exact 2011–2014 ISM-filtered data, and the NCI (Matthews) group released GGIR-processed day-level sleep output for all of NHANES 2011–2014. Use one of these rather than inventing a pipeline.

5. **The shared-device problem is real and acknowledged in the field but rarely solved.** The cleanest mitigation is to define light metrics relative to individually-estimated sleep timing and to exclude the sleep period from daytime light metrics, then show robustness. This is exactly the manoeuvre that also creates the "using sleep to define light windows while treating sleep as an outcome" conflict — meaning you cannot have both a clean light exposure and a clean sleep outcome from one device without explicit, disclosed trade-offs.

## Details

### A. Closest prior work

**PAXLUX papers (how many, what they did, validation).** In adults: effectively none using lux as a circadian exposure. The identifiable light-sensor analyses are in the 2012 NNYFS children (PMC9040315), which used lux to classify time outdoors, citing Flynn et al. for indoor/outdoor discrimination — not circadian light dosimetry. A muscle-mass NHANES 2011–2014 paper ("outdoor light exposure and low muscle mass," Medicine/Ovid) uses the lux channel but as a crude "outdoor light" proxy, not a validated melanopic exposure. Net: the lux sensor has not been validated against reference photometry in NHANES, and no adult paper has built day/night light-exposure metrics from it. Your light arm is novel and its methods must be imported.

**NHANES-derived IS/IV/RA/M10/L5 (Tang 2024 as precedent).** Confirmed via full text: Tang et al. used **NHANES 2013–2014 only**, N=7,410, with **53 epilepsy cases (0.75%)**. Epilepsy was defined from the prescription-medication file: participants self-reporting a medication for "epilepsy and recurrent seizures" (ICD-10-CM **G40**), with manual confirmation that the drug was a standard ASM. Rhythm indices (IS, IV, RA, L5, M10, and L5/M10 start times) were computed with the **nparACT** R package from minute-level MIMS (PAXMTSM). They weighted with **WTINT2YR** and the R **survey** package but **did not name SDMVSTRA or SDMVPSU**, and WTINT2YR (the interview weight) is arguably the wrong weight for accelerometer (MEC-linked) data. Findings: PWE had lower IS and M10; higher IV and later M10 start associated with higher epilepsy prevalence. Related NHANES rest–activity precedents: Li et al. (2022, *Front Endocrinol*, obesity) used NHANES 2011–2014 (N=7,838), extended cosinor + nonparametric IS/IV.

**Diary-free NHANES sleep derivations.** Su et al. (2022, *Sci Rep*, PMC9090869) derived sleep for 11,279 participants (≥3 valid days; valid day >16 h wear) using an unsupervised **Hidden Markov Model** sleep–wake classifier, taking the longest sleep block in a noon–noon window as the sleep-period time; they masked minutes flagged as nonwear (PAXPREDM), quality-flagged (PAXQFM>0) or uncomputable MIMS (−0.01). Thapa-Chhetry et al. (2022, *MSSE* 54(11):1936–1946) built and PSG-validated (n=21 PSG; n=31 observed wake-wear; n=20 nonwear) a classifier specifically for the 2011–2014 ISM-filtered data (F1 0.84 sleep/wake; 0.94 wake/nonwear). The NCI/Matthews "Monitoring Sleep Duration, Timing, and Continuity … Using Actigraphy" release (DataLumos) provides **GGIR-processed** day-level NHANES sleep output. None of these used SLQ self-report as the sleep-detection input (there is no diary to use).

**Multi-domain single-wrist-device cohorts.** UK Biobank is the dominant template: Burns et al. (2023, *Nat Mental Health* 1(11):853–862) analysed light, sleep, physical activity and psychiatric disorders in **86,772 adults (57% women; mean age 62.4 ± 7.4 y)** — "the largest to date cross-sectional analysis of light, sleep, physical activity, and mental health"; Windred et al. (2024, *Lancet Reg Health Eur* 42:100943) linked day/night light to incident type 2 diabetes in **84,790 people (62.3 ± 7.9 y, 58% female; 1,997 incident cases over 7.9 ± 1.2 y), ~13 million hours of light data**; Windred et al. (2024, *PNAS* 121(43):e2405924121) linked light to mortality in **88,905 people (62.4 ± 7.8 y, 57% female; 3,750 deaths over 8.0 ± 1.0 y)**. These derived day/night light by factor analysis of 24-h light profiles and modelled circadian amplitude/phase with a mathematical pacemaker model — a framework directly transferable despite UK Biobank's different (Axivity) sensor.

**UK Biobank light-methods group (Windred/Burns/Cain).** Their pipeline — factor-analytic day/night light exposure, thresholded time-above metrics, sleepreg-based SRI (Windred et al. 2021, *Sleep*, "Objective assessment of sleep regularity in 60,000 UK Biobank participants") — is the most citable transferable methodology for the light and regularity arms.

### B. Cohort definition

**Epilepsy ascertainment.** NHANES 2011–2014 has **no direct epilepsy/seizure item** — the Medical Conditions questionnaire (MCQ_G/MCQ_H) does not ask about epilepsy or seizures. Ascertainment therefore relies on the prescription file (RXQ_RX_G/RXQ_RX_H) using the participant-reported reason-for-use ICD-10-CM codes (RXDRSC1–3/RXDRSD1) mapped to **G40**, plus manual confirmation the drug is a true ASM (Multum therapeutic class). This is exactly what Tang et al. (2024) and Terman et al. (2020, *Epilepsy Behav* 111:107261, PMC7869064) did. Terman found 135 ASM-for-G40 users among 20,146 (2013–2016; weighted 0.8%, **representing 2,399,520 US citizens** via NHANES's sampling frame), explicitly blanking G40-coded non-ASMs (hydrocodone, allopurinol, apixaban).

**Validation/misclassification.** Terman et al. cite Brooks et al. (2012, *Epilepsy Behav* 23:57–63) — self-reported epilepsy for community surveillance had **PPV 74%, sensitivity 84%** — and Holden et al. (2005, *Dis Manag* 8:1–14) that requiring an ASM improves case detection. The reason-code (G40) requirement is the main safeguard against false positives from off-label ASM use (gabapentin/pregabalin for pain, topiramate for migraine, valproate/lamotrigine for bipolar disorder): a gabapentin "for nerve pain" carries a pain ICD code, not G40. No NHANES-specific study quantifies residual misclassification from off-label ASM use; this is a genuine gap to flag. Recommended sensitivity analyses: (i) restrict to "narrow" ASMs rarely used off-label (e.g., levetiracetam, phenytoin, carbamazepine, lacosamide) vs a "broad" definition; (ii) require ≥1 ASM AND a G40 reason; (iii) vary whether people on ASMs coded to non-epilepsy indications are excluded or reclassified.

**Expected case count.** Plan for ~40–60 accelerometry-valid PWE across both cycles combined (Tang found 53 in one cycle; valid-accelerometry restriction will shrink this). This is a small-n exposure group; comparable small-exposure NHANES papers (Terman; Tang) rely on quartile/binary predictor categorisation and present weighted proportions with wide CIs rather than finely-adjusted continuous models.

**ASM as both ascertainment and confounder (circularity).** ASMs independently affect sleepiness, sleep architecture and activity, yet ASM use *is* the exposure-defining variable — so you cannot adjust for "any ASM" without adjusting away the exposure. Escape routes: (i) treat ASM *burden* (polytherapy count, specific sedating agents such as benzodiazepines/barbiturates) as a covariate/effect-modifier *within* the epilepsy group in a secondary analysis, not in the case-vs-control contrast; (ii) present the case-control contrast as the total effect of "treated epilepsy" and state plainly that medication effects are part of that estimand; (iii) stratify by monotherapy vs polytherapy. Do not adjust for the ascertainment variable in the primary model.

### C. Sleep data — what actually exists

- **Self-report (SLQ_G/SLQ_H):** only SLD010H (weekday/workday sleep hours), SLQ050, SLQ060. No timing, no weekend framing, no diary. Self-reported duration is known to be overestimated versus objective measures, and the weekday-only framing precludes social-jetlag computation. Model SLD010H as an *auxiliary* variable: agreement/disagreement between self-report and accelerometer-derived duration is itself informative, and PWE may systematically misreport — a legitimate secondary analysis (Bland–Altman; regress discrepancy on epilepsy).
- **No diary alongside PAM:** documented in the PAM protocol ("No activity logs, diaries, or records were kept"). This restricts you to diary-free algorithms (van Hees HDCZA; GGIR L5±6h guider; the Thapa-Chhetry NHANES classifier; HMM).
- **24-h continuous wear:** yes — participants wore the ActiGraph GT3X+ 24 h/day for 7 days; mean ~1,437 of 1,440 valid minutes on non-partial days; ~96% wore to day 9. First and last days are partial and conventionally dropped. Overnight non-wear is distinguished from sleep only algorithmically (PAXPREDM's sleep vs nonwear categories, or a validated classifier) — the central hazard for a sleep arm.

### D. Deriving sleep from accelerometry

- **Algorithms:** van Hees HDCZA (2018, no-diary) is the field standard for raw wrist data and underpins GGIR; the GGIR L5±6h guider is the diary-free default. Cole-Kripke and Sadeh were validated on ActiGraph counts (not raw MIMS/ENMO) and are not appropriate for NHANES MIMS without recalibration. The **Thapa-Chhetry classifier is the only one PSG-validated on the actual NHANES 2011–2014 ISM-filtered signal** and is the most defensible choice; the HMM approach (Su 2022) and GGIR (NCI release) are acceptable published alternatives.
- **MIMS-specific validation:** none of the classic count-based algorithms were validated on MIMS. This is a real substitution cost: any count-based scoring applied to MIMS must be caveated as unvalidated on this unit. Prefer the NHANES-native (Thapa-Chhetry) or raw-ENMO GGIR pipelines.
- **Reliably derivable metrics:** sleep-period time, total sleep time, sleep onset/offset timing, and sleep efficiency are defensible; WASO and sleep-percentage are moderately reliable. **Sleep onset latency is unreliable from actigraphy** (documented: actigraphy has good sensitivity for sleep, poor specificity for wake) and should be avoided. SRI (via the sleepreg package; Windred 2021) is derivable and validated (also in MESA older adults; Lunsford-Avery et al. 2018, *Sci Rep*) but needs ≥5 overlapping days. Follow the RIRI reporting statement for any regularity index.
- **Napping/multiple episodes:** most pipelines take the longest sleep block in a noon–noon window as the main sleep period (Su 2022), which discards naps; GGIR can retain daytime sleep. State your rule; daytime napping inflates IV and should be handled consistently across the rhythm and sleep arms.
- **Objective vs self-report:** model both; disagreement is informative given likely PWE misreporting (see C).

### E. Preprocessing

- **Valid-day/valid-night criteria (consensus range):** published NHANES 2011–2014 papers use ≥3 or ≥4 valid days; Li (2022) required ≥4 valid 24-h periods for rhythm metrics; Su (2022) required ≥3 valid days with >16 h wear for sleep. A defensible standard: ≥4 valid days for rhythm/light metrics and ≥3 valid nights for sleep, dropping partial first/last days. **The reconciliation problem is real:** rhythm/light metrics use valid *days* while sleep uses valid *nights*, producing different inclusion sets. To avoid selection bias, define one analytic sample meeting the union of criteria for the primary multi-domain models and report domain-specific samples as sensitivity analyses; document the overlap.
- **Flags:** standard exclusions are PAXMTSM = −0.01 (uncomputable), PAXQFM>0 (quality flag), and PAXPREDM = "nonwear." The contested point is **PAXPREDM's "sleep" category**: it is a *predicted* label and should **not** be used directly as your sleep outcome without independent validation — Thapa-Chhetry showed sleep-vs-wake classification on this signal is imperfect (F1 ≈0.84). Use PAXPREDM for nonwear masking; use a validated classifier for the sleep *outcome*.
- **MIMS vs raw:** NHANES released minute-level MIMS (PAXMIN_G/H, variable PAXMTSM) and 1-Hz-summarised lux (PAXLXMM); raw 80-Hz data exist but are large/partly RDC-restricted. Almost all published work uses the released MIMS; reprocessing raw 80-Hz is defensible only if you need ENMO for GGIR sleep and should be justified.
- **PAXLUX handling:** lux is the mean of 1-Hz readings per minute (PAXLXMM in PAXMIN). **Saturation ceiling: values at or above 2,500 lux were coded as "2,500 lux"** (per CDC NHANES/NNYFS PAXLUX documentation; device = ActiGraph GT3X+, acceleration at 80 Hz and ambient light at 1 Hz) — this censors bright outdoor exposure and biases daytime metrics downward; acknowledge and, ideally, model daytime light as time-above-threshold rather than mean. Zero/near-zero night values are massively inflated (wrist under bedding) — use log(x+1) or the Zauner et al. (2025, *J Biol Rhythms*) zero-inflated-on-log approach rather than naive log. The sensor is **uncalibrated photopic lux, never validated against reference photometry in NHANES**, and cannot be converted to melanopic EDI.

### F. Light metrics

- **Field reporting standard:** CIE S 026 melanopic EDI (Brown et al. 2022 thresholds: mel-EDI <10 lux pre-bed, >250 lux day) and the Spitschan V3 framework (verification/analytical/clinical validation; Spitschan et al. 2022, *Digit Health*) plus the emerging RIRI/LightLogR reporting conventions. **You cannot compute melanopic units from NHANES photopic lux** — you must justify raw photopic lux explicitly, cite the top-coding and lack of spectral information as limitations, and avoid implying melanopic interpretation.
- **Conventional metrics (defensible on photopic lux):** mean daytime and nighttime lux, time above threshold (100, 250, 1000 lux — note top-coding weakens the ≥1000-lux contrasts), MLiT (mean light timing), brightest-continuous-10h and darkest-continuous-5h of light (light analogues of M10/L5), day–night light contrast, and interdaily stability of light. Hartmeyer & Andersen catalogued >40 such metrics; LightLogR implements 61 metrics across 17 families.
- **Sleep-relative vs clock-time windows:** light defined relative to individual sleep timing (pre-sleep-onset light, light during the sleep period, post-wake light) is established in the real-world light literature and is **preferable in principle** here because PWE may have shifted sleep timing — fixed clock windows would confound "less nighttime light" with "went to bed later." However, sleep-anchored windows conflict with treating sleep as an outcome (see I.4). Recommended resolution: use fixed clock-time windows for the primary light-vs-epilepsy analysis and reserve sleep-anchored windows for descriptive/sensitivity purposes.
- **Packages:** LightLogR (Zauner, Hartmeyer & Spitschan 2025, *JOSS* 10(107):7601; validated, actively maintained, field standard) for light metrics; LuxPy and pyplr exist but are less used for epidemiological metric extraction. GGIR has a light module but it was designed for GENEActiv/Axivity spectral channels, not top-coded photopic NHANES lux — do not assume its light defaults transfer.

### G. Activity and circadian metrics

- **GGIR vs nparACT vs bespoke:** nparACT (Blume et al. 2016, *MethodsX*) is the transparent standard for IS/IV/RA/M10/L5 and was used by the direct precedent (Tang 2024); GGIR produces the same family plus sleep and light in one pipeline. Implementations differ materially in binning, handling of missing minutes and whether full-day restriction is applied — report the package, version and settings.
- **Nonparametric vs cosinor vs extended cosinor:** nonparametric (IS/IV/RA) makes no waveform assumption and is preferred for fragmented/non-sinusoidal rhythms (likely in PWE); cosinor assumes a sinusoid (often violated); extended cosinor (anti-logistic, used by Li 2022) is a compromise. Report nonparametric as primary; cosinor as sensitivity.
- **MIMS vs ActiGraph-count comparability:** IS/IV/RA computed on MIMS are **not numerically identical** to those from ActiGraph counts in the older literature — MIMS is a different unit, so absolute values are not comparable across devices even if rank-order patterns are. State this; do not benchmark your MIMS-based IS against count-based norms.
- **GGIR one-pipeline:** yes, GGIR produces activity, sleep and (with a suitable sensor) light in one validated pipeline — but its light module is not designed for top-coded photopic single-channel lux, so a hybrid (GGIR for activity/sleep, LightLogR for light) is more defensible.

### H. Statistics

- **Complex survey design:** the accelerometry subsample is an MEC-linked component, so **WTMEC2YR** is the correct base weight (CDC "least common denominator" rule), combined across cycles by halving (WTMEC2YR/2 for the 4-year 2011–2014 sample), with SDMVSTRA (strata) and SDMVPSU (PSU) and Taylor linearisation (R survey / svydesign; Stata svy). Note CDC has **not released a PAM-specific weight**, so WTMEC2YR is the accepted proxy — flag as a judgement call. Many wearable-NHANES papers ignore weights or use the wrong weight (Tang used WTINT2YR); this has been criticised and you should not repeat it. Degrees of freedom = (#PSUs − #strata); with ~50 cases spread across PSUs, subgroup df are small — use conservative CIs and consider degrees-of-freedom adjustments.
- **Propensity matching + weights (contested):** guidance exists but is not settled. DuGoff et al. (2014), Austin et al. (2018), Lenis et al. (2019) and Zanutto (2006), synthesised in "It's all about balance" (*Biostatistics* 2019), recommend that if you match/weight on propensity scores in survey data you must **include the survey weight as a covariate in the PS model and carry survey weights into the outcome model** ("double adjustment"). Pure PSM that discards weights forfeits national representativeness. **Recommendation:** do not combine PSM with survey weights in the primary analysis; instead use survey-weighted regression with covariate adjustment (primary), and offer a separate *unweighted* propensity-matched sensitivity analysis clearly labelled as estimating a sample (not national) effect — the two answer different questions and should not be forced together.
- **Confounders vs mediators.** Confounders (adjust): age, sex, race/ethnicity, poverty–income ratio, education, employment/shift work, season (RIDEXMON), and depression only if treated as a pre-existing comorbidity. Ambiguous/likely mediators (do NOT adjust in the total-effect model; explore separately): physical activity/M10 (a mediator when light→activity→sleep), sleep itself (a mediator if light→sleep), BMI (downstream of rhythm disruption), and depression (PHQ-9 — plausibly a mediator of epilepsy→sleep). ASM use: ascertainment variable, not a covariate (see B). Adjusting for a mediator (activity, sleep, BMI) induces collider/overadjustment bias — the same warning your brief raises now applies to sleep.
- **Three correlated outcome families.** IS, IV, RA, M10, sleep efficiency and sleep timing are strongly intercorrelated. Options: (i) pre-register a small number of primary endpoints per family and apply family-wise correction (e.g., one light, one rhythm, one sleep primary), the rest exploratory; (ii) reduce dimensionality first (factor analysis or PCA of the rhythm/light block, as UK Biobank light papers did) and model factor scores; (iii) avoid a single omnibus MANOVA that obscures which domain drives effects. **Recommended:** dimensionality reduction within the light and rhythm blocks + a few pre-specified primaries with FDR control — this avoids both over-correction and fishing.
- **Mediation and causal ordering.** Cross-sectional mediation is biased for the underlying longitudinal process — Maxwell & Cole (2007, *Psychol Methods* 12:23–44) and Maxwell, Cole & Mitchell (2011, *Multivariate Behav Res* 46:816–841) show cross-sectional estimates of indirect effects can be substantially wrong even under partial mediation. There is **no cross-sectional basis to order light→circadian→sleep over light→sleep→circadian or a reciprocal loop** — these are observationally equivalent in one week of data. Careful papers therefore (a) abandon "mediation" language for **"attenuation"** ("the epilepsy coefficient attenuated by X% when light was added"), which reviewers accept; (b) if formal mediation is attempted, run it under *both* orderings as a sensitivity analysis (CMAverse; Shi et al. 2021) and report E-values (VanderWeele & Ding 2017; the evalue package and CMAverse::cmsens) for unmeasured mediator–outcome confounding; and (c) never claim the ordering is identified. The phrase "the epilepsy coefficient shrinks when light is added" is acceptable **only** as descriptive attenuation, not as evidence of mediation.
- **Power.** With ~50 cases vs several thousand controls, power is dominated by the case count; you can reliably detect only moderate-to-large standardised differences (roughly Cohen's d ≥ 0.4–0.5 at 80% power, two-sided α=0.05, before survey design-effect inflation). Comparable small-exposure NHANES papers (Terman; Tang) responded by categorising predictors, reporting weighted prevalences with wide CIs, and framing findings as hypothesis-generating. Provide a design-effect-adjusted power statement and pre-register it. For the light arm specifically, the hierarchical-bootstrap power framework of Guidolin/Spitschan (PLOS One 2024, "Power analysis for personal light exposure measurements") is the appropriate citation.

### I. Combination-defensibility conflicts (do not treat these as independently solvable)

1. **Survey weights + propensity matching:** conflicting unless double-adjusted; keep them in separate analyses answering separate estimands (see H).
2. **Three-way shared-device tautology:** low nighttime lux is partly a restatement of "asleep" (wrist under bedding), and the light–activity association is partly mechanical. Mitigations used in the field: exclude the algorithm-defined sleep period from daytime light metrics; report light metrics both including and excluding the sleep period; run a "waking-hours-only" light analysis; and explicitly acknowledge occlusion. There is no sleeve/occlusion correction available for NHANES. Disclose that the light–activity–sleep associations are partly non-independent by construction.
3. **Adjusting for activity or sleep when they are mediators:** overadjustment. Keep them out of the total-effect model; use them as outcomes or in clearly-labelled mediator analyses only.
4. **Using sleep-derived timing to define light windows while treating sleep as an outcome:** direct conflict. If light windows are anchored to sleep onset/offset, the light exposure and the sleep outcome share estimation error and are not independent. Resolution: either (a) use fixed clock-time light windows for the light-as-outcome/light-vs-epilepsy analysis and reserve sleep-anchored windows for descriptive purposes, or (b) if sleep-anchored windows are primary, do not simultaneously treat the same sleep-timing variable as an outcome — pick one role per variable and state it.
5. **Melanopic-calibrated tools on uncalibrated photopic lux:** LightLogR/CIE metrics assume calibrated (ideally melanopic) input; applying them to top-coded photopic NHANES lux is defensible only for *relative* photopic metrics with explicit caveats — never report the output as melanopic EDI.

## Recommendations

**Stage 1 — Primary analysis (conservative, survey-weighted; two co-primary outcome families — light and rest–activity rhythm; sleep secondary).**
- *Inclusion:* adults ≥20 (or ≥16 to match SLQ eligibility, stated), both cycles combined; epilepsy = ASM with G40 reason (RXQ_RX), manual ASM confirmation; ≥4 valid days (drop partial first/last), nonwear masked via PAXPREDM + PAXQFM>0 + PAXMTSM=−0.01.
- *Metrics:* Rhythm — IS, IV, RA, M10, L5 (nparACT on PAXMTSM), nonparametric primary. Light — mean daytime lux, time above 250 lux (day), mean nighttime lux/time above 10 lux (night, log(x+1) or Zauner zero-inflated handling), day–night contrast, from PAXLXMM using **fixed clock-time windows** (to keep light independent of sleep). Sleep (secondary) — SPT, TST, sleep efficiency, sleep midpoint via the Thapa-Chhetry NHANES-validated classifier or GGIR/HMM.
- *Model:* survey-weighted regression, WTMEC2YR/2, SDMVSTRA, SDMVPSU, Taylor linearisation; confounders = age, sex, race/ethnicity, PIR, education, employment/shift work, season. Dimensionality reduction within light and rhythm blocks; ≤3 pre-specified primaries with FDR control.
- *Causal ordering:* none assumed; report attenuation of the epilepsy coefficient when light is added, plus E-values.
- *Sensitivity:* narrow vs broad epilepsy definition; ≥3 vs ≥5 valid days; light metrics including vs excluding the sleep period; unweighted analysis.
- *Justifying citations:* Tang 2024 (rhythm precedent), Li 2022 (extended cosinor), Blume 2016 (nparACT), Zauner 2025 (light handling/LightLogR), Thapa-Chhetry 2022 (sleep), CDC analytic guidelines + weighting tutorial (weights), VanderWeele & Ding 2017 (E-values).

**Stage 2 — Matched-cohort sensitivity (sample-level estimand).**
- Unweighted 1:k propensity matching (age, sex, race, PIR, education, season, depression-as-comorbidity), balance diagnostics; same outcome metrics. Labelled explicitly as a sample (not national) effect; do NOT carry survey weights. Justify with DuGoff 2014 / Austin 2018 / "It's all about balance" 2019.

**Stage 3 — Exploratory attenuation/mediation (clearly caveated).**
- CMAverse under BOTH orderings (light→rhythm→sleep and light→sleep→rhythm), E-values via cmsens; report as exploratory, non-identified, using "attenuation" language (Maxwell & Cole 2007).

**On whether to add sleep:** **Add it only as a secondary/exploratory domain, not a co-primary third outcome family.** It imports (i) a fresh circularity (sleep-anchored light windows vs sleep-as-outcome), (ii) an unvalidated-on-MIMS scoring step for some algorithms, and (iii) a heavily intercorrelated outcome family that will consume power and multiplicity budget while adding little independent information beyond IS/IV/RA (which already capture rest–activity structure). The strongest, cleanest paper is light + rest–activity rhythm as co-primaries, with sleep as a supporting secondary analysis and the objective-vs-self-report discrepancy as a novel secondary angle.

**Benchmarks that would change the recommendation:** if you obtain RDC access to the raw 1-Hz lux and 80-Hz accelerometry, sleep-anchored light metrics and raw-ENMO GGIR sleep become more defensible and sleep could be elevated. If the valid-accelerometry case count exceeds ~100 (e.g., by pooling additional indirect ascertainment), continuous adjusted models and formal mediation gain credibility.

## Caveats
- **NHANES-specific evidence is thin for the light arm:** no adult PAXLUX precedent and no reference-photometry validation of the sensor; the entire light methodology is borrowed from UK Biobank (Windred/Burns/Cain) and the Spitschan/CIE literature, on a top-coded (≥2,500 lux) uncalibrated photopic sensor that cannot yield melanopic units.
- **Sleep timing is not measurable from NHANES self-report** (no bedtime/waketime before 2015–2016; no diary), so every sleep-timing metric depends on an accelerometer algorithm whose sleep/wake specificity is imperfect (Thapa-Chhetry F1 ≈0.84).
- **Small n (~50 cases)** limits power to moderate/large effects and makes finely-adjusted continuous models unstable; treat as hypothesis-generating.
- **The precedent (Tang 2024) used the interview weight (WTINT2YR) and did not report SDMVSTRA/SDMVPSU** — do not treat its statistical approach as a template; the correct base weight is WTMEC2YR.
- **Cross-sectional mediation is not identifiable here;** any causal-ordering claim (light→circadian→sleep) is unsupported and should be replaced with attenuation language and dual-ordering sensitivity analyses.
- **Every "consensus" preprocessing threshold is a judgement call** (valid-day count, wear-hour minimum, nap handling, log transform, threshold choices) and must be pre-registered and defended; differing valid-day vs valid-night sets must be reconciled into one analytic sample to avoid selection bias.
- **Points not fully verifiable from primary documentation:** whether Tang et al. declared SDMVSTRA/SDMVPSU (not stated in their text); the exact valid-accelerometry PWE count you will obtain across both cycles (depends on your ascertainment and valid-day rules). No dedicated NHANES-specific study quantifies residual epilepsy misclassification from off-label ASM use — the closest anchors are Brooks et al. (2012) general self-report validation figures (PPV 74%, sensitivity 84%).
